This is the Boone River basin in Iowa from April to mid-September 2025, with a fire forced on 5 September to see what a shock does to the plants and animals. Drag the bar to move through the months, or press play.

Rain and snow from the ERA5 reanalysis fall on the basin, soak into the soil or run off, and reach the river gauge at Webster City, where the model can be compared with what the US Geological Survey measured.

A deer herd grazes the plants and a cohort of predators eats the deer. Both are accounts that must close: every animal is born, dies or is still there.

A fire is forced on 5 September 2025 as a shock. It did not happen. The point is to watch what the plants and animals do afterwards.

Burning now

The run ends two weeks after the fire, so it shows the shock and not the recovery, and a season this short cannot separate recovery from autumn.

  • The river and the greenness are checked against measurements. The animals are not: no count of deer or predators in this basin is used.
  • The starting densities, six deer and 0.3 predators per square kilometre, are set in the manifest and do not come from a survey.
  • These are early rungs of the engine's ladder, kept as checks that the accounts close across parts.
