This is one run of the Portland scenario: a magnitude 9 earthquake on the Cascadia subduction zone at 2 p.m. on Monday 1 February 2027, followed to the end of the year. Drag the bar under the picture to move through the year, or press play. Everything on this page follows the moment the bar shows.

The run tells its own story as it goes. Each sentence below is written by the part of the model that worked the thing out, at the moment it happened, with its source underneath. The newest is first.

This is an early model. Several of its results disagree with the studies they can be checked against, and many of its numbers are recalled from memory or guessed. The last tab says which.

The model estimates, for each 30-metre cell, what share of the buildings take extensive or complete damage, and draws at random how many collapse. Nearly all of the deaths in the model happen in collapsed buildings, so the toll depends on which buildings the draw brings down.

The two death counts should agree and in daytime runs they do not. The cause has not been found, so neither should be quoted as this model's toll. The total at the top of the page uses the count by agent.

The fuel terminals in northwest Portland are both the spill in the first minutes and the reserve in the months after. Each tank fails or holds by a draw against a fragility curve, and a failed tank releases a share of its contents that depends on its age.

Where it meets the river

The reserve cannot yet be read off this chart. The model starts the emergency response's fuel use only when a truck can reach a hospital by road, which in most runs is months away, and the daily use and the day resupply resumes are guesses. The model also does not move the fuel once it reaches the river, and it has no fire.

Each of the twelve bridges over the Willamette is damaged or spared by a draw against the curve for its class, and reopens after a repair time set by its damage. The emergency routes close where the ground is expected to deform. Each day the model asks which hospitals a truck could reach from the fuel terminals over open roads and open bridges.

The curve for the ten older bridges is this project's judgement, anchored on a county report. The days a road stays closed are guesses.

Electricity in the model depends only on transmission towers and poles. A neighbourhood has power when its nearest structures stand, and crews repair twelve a day. A hospital without grid power runs its generators for 72 hours and then needs a fuel delivery.

Where the power is out

This is the model's least credible result. It has no substations, lines or neighbourhood distribution, so power returns in weeks where the state expects one to three months. The number of hospitals that lose power, and the deaths in the months after, are too low for the same reason.

  • The ground shaking is read from the state geology agency's published maps. The model does not simulate the fault.
  • Every fragility curve and casualty rate is recalled from memory of FEMA's Hazus manual and has not been checked against it.
  • The death toll from the shaking is fitted to the state's estimate, and the model's two ways of counting it disagree by day.
  • The spill comes out below the minimum of the 2022 study of the terminals.
  • Electricity returns too fast, and what depends on it is too mild.
  • Drinking water follows an assumed schedule and is not computed.
  • There is no fire, no evacuation, no debris on the roads and nobody leaves the city.

The project page gives the reasons and the sources, and Limits today keeps the list current.
