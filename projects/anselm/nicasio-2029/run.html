This is one run of an imagined fire: it starts at Devil's Gulch campsite in Marin County on 1 October 2029 and is followed for three days under the wind of a real Red Flag day, 27 to 29 October 2019. Drag the bar under the picture to move through the run, or press play. Everything on this page follows the moment the bar shows.

The model divides the ground into 30-metre cells. Each cell has a height, a land cover and, where there are any, buildings. A burning cell heats its neighbours, more strongly downwind and uphill, and a neighbour catches when it has taken enough heat. Embers can start new fires ahead of the front.

What the model takes each cell to be made of

Two places are staked on the map: the campsite where the fire starts and the house at 550 Nicasio Valley Road. Each hour the model measures how far the nearest burned ground is from each.

The house is placed by the Census geocoder, which puts an address on the road and not on the building. The model counts a place as reached when fire is within 50 metres.

Each box is a module and an arrow is something one module writes and another reads. The relief, the land cover, the roads and the buildings are each read once from their source. The weather lays each hour's air on the cells and the fuel moisture module turns that air into how dry the dead fuel is. The fire reads all of them and fetches nothing itself. The animals read where the fire has burned, and the records score it.

The parts were one module until 26 November 2026. Separating them changed none of the fire's numbers. Heat passing between cells, ignition and burning out are still one computation inside the fire, because they cannot be pulled apart without changing what is computed.

A placeholder population, declared as one: twenty animals on every square kilometre of grass, shrub and tree at the start. Each hour the animals on newly burned ground move to the nearest unburned cell, a tenth of them die there, and those with no unburned ground within three kilometres die. Every animal is accounted for.

Animals per square kilometre

This says nothing about any real species. An animal here moves only once its own cell has burned and goes to the nearest unburned cell, a road or a town included. It is in the run to show that a second module can react to the fire without the fire knowing it exists.

The wind and the dryness of dead fuel drive the spread. Both are read from weather records and are the same in every variant of the scenario unless the variant says otherwise.

  • Every constant of the fire engine is a guess. One of them, the multiplier on the heat a cell needs before it catches, is the knob the variants turn.
  • Nobody fights the fire unless the variant says so.
  • The wind does not bend around the terrain, and the fire does not change the wind.
  • The fuel table is recalled from memory of Scott and Burgan (2005) and has not been checked against it.
  • There is no record to check this fire against, because it has not happened.
  • The variants disagree completely: with the catching threshold times 3 or 5 the fire takes the whole window, and times 8 it never leaves its first cell. The engine cannot say which is right.
  • The wind is borrowed from another year's storm.
