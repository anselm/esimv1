This is one run of an imagined fire on Rothermel's spread equations: it starts at Devil's Gulch campsite in Marin County on 1 October 2029 and is followed for three days under the weather of 27 to 29 October 2019. Drag the bar under the map to move through the run, or press play. Everything on this page follows the moment the bar shows.

This model spreads fire by Rothermel's equations, the ones behind the fire-behaviour tools agencies use: a rate of spread for each cell from its fuel, its slope, the wind and the dryness of the fuel.

The fuel model assigned to each cell

Two places are staked on the map: the campsite where the fire starts and the house at 550 Nicasio Valley Road. Each hour the model measures how far the nearest burned ground is from each.

In all four variants this model stops between 1.3 and 2.2 kilometres short of the house. The same scenario on our own engine either reaches it within a day and a half or never spreads at all. The two models disagree and there is no record to choose between them.

Each box is a module and an arrow is something one module writes and another reads. The relief and the land cover are each read once from their source. The weather lays each hour's air on the cells and the fuel moisture module turns that air into how dry the dead fuel is. Rothermel's model reads all of them and fetches nothing itself. The animals read where the fire is and when each cell caught, and the places module watches the house.

The fire was one module that loaded its own ground and weather until 26 November 2026. After the separation the burned area was the same at every hour. The relief, land cover, weather and fuel moisture here are the same modules that the first Devil's Gulch page reads with a different fire model in this one's place.

A placeholder population, declared as one: twenty animals on every square kilometre of tree, shrub, grass and wetland at the start. They notice newly burning ground within 600 metres and move away from it at three kilometres an hour. The hour is replayed in five-minute steps from the fire's own record of when each cell caught, so the animals move ahead of the front and not after it. Of those the front overtakes, three in ten die and the rest reach the nearest unburned ground. Ground holding more than four times its starting number sheds the excess to the ground around it, a few cells each hour. An animal driven to the edge of the modelled area leaves it. Every animal is accounted for.

Animals per square kilometre

This says nothing about any real species, and none of these numbers is an estimate. The distance, the speed and the share that dies are guesses with no source, and the animals cross towns and roads as if they were open ground. The module is in the run to show that a part can react to the fire without the fire being changed: it reads where the fire is and when each cell caught, and nothing else.

The weather is one point of the ERA5 reanalysis, hour by hour, for the whole window, laid on the ground by the weather module. In the gusts variant the weather module writes the hourly gust as the wind.

  • The fuel table is recalled from memory of Scott and Burgan (2005) and has not been checked against it.
  • The wind is one point of a coarse reanalysis and does not bend around the terrain.
  • Nobody fights the fire unless the variant says so.
  • This model writes no elevation frame, so its run page has a flat map where the other fire pages have a relief.
  • There is no record to check this fire against, because it has not happened.
  • The house is placed by the Census geocoder, on the road and not on the building.
