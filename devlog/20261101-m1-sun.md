# 2026-11-01 — M1 Sun: clear-sky irradiance over the Boone River, energy ledger closes

Anselm: "Let's go for it." Work by Claude (Fable 5.1) in this room, session
https://claude.ai/code/session_018TGCFhEpYs9hm68xDLd5Xy, on canada1. Dates: the session
straddled midnight UTC; it is still 2026-10-31 in San Francisco.

## What runs

`manifests/m1-sun-boone.toml`: one year (2025) of hourly clear-sky irradiance over a
window 42.3 to 43.0 N, 94.0 to 93.5 W around the Boone River gauge, on the Copernicus
DEM aggregated to 15 arc-seconds (120 by 168 cells, about 450 m). Release build, single
thread: 8,760 steps in 19.6 s, 365 daily ledger closes with zero leak, 34 MB of output
including a daily-mean irradiance frame per day. `esim verify` passes: two runs give one
digest, and a run resumed from a mid-year snapshot reproduces the log tail exactly.
`manifests/m1-sun-boone-week.toml` is the quick June version at 30 arc-seconds.

Numbers, for the record and for the scoreboard that arrives at M4:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| peak clear-sky GHI, 21 June 2025, domain mean | 948 W/m2 just after local solar noon |
| daily mean irradiance on the slope, 21 June | 347 W/m2 |
| annual mean, 2025 | 219 W/m2 |
| 1 January / 1 April / 1 July / 1 October daily means | 84 / 244 / 346 / 203 W/m2 |
| energy through the top of the atmosphere, June week | 9.4e17 J; 72% reached the surface, 28% to the atmosphere, 0.00002% blocked by terrain |

The June peak and daily mean are what a clear-sky model should give at this latitude.
The annual mean is clear-sky, so it sits above the real value, which cloud will bring
down once weather arrives (M4). Terrain shadow is negligible because Iowa is flat
(maximum slope in the window 1.7 degrees); the mechanism is in and will matter elsewhere.

## What was built

- Kernel: a lat-lon grid with true spherical cell areas (tested: a 10-degree grid sums
  to the sphere to 1e-9), field snapshot series in the context, field frames written per
  run as little-endian float32 files with metadata in `run.json`.
- Registry: `dem_window` aggregates the one-arc-second tiles that cover a box to a chosen
  resolution by block mean; Horn slope and aspect; horizon angles in 16 azimuths by ray
  marching to 20 km; every derived layer cached in `data/cache/derived/` keyed by the
  tile hashes, the box, the resolution and a recipe version. Anselm: "No point in
  fetching basic reference data twice." Right, and the same holds for computing a
  derived layer twice; both are now once per box.
- Module `sun.irradiance`: NOAA (Spencer 1971) solar position, Kasten and Young (1989)
  air mass, Meinel clear-sky direct normal irradiance, one tenth diffuse, incidence on
  the slope, horizon shadowing, and an energy ledger in joules whose `surface` stock is
  declared every step. Three unit tests on solar geometry (solstice noon zenith at the
  gauge within 0.6 degrees of latitude minus declination).
- GDAL 3.8.4 installed with apt as a data-preparation and cross-check tool, after
  Anselm asked about it. Not a dependency of any crate. It immediately earned its keep:
  `gdalinfo -stats` reproduced our reader's min, max and mean to three decimals, and
  `gdallocationinfo` at the gauge disagreed with us by ten metres, which exposed that
  Copernicus tiles declare pixel-is-point coordinates. The reader now honours GeoKey 1025
  and reads 310.1 m where GDAL reads 310.07 m. The gauge datum (gauge zero) is 301.6 m.
- Viewer: a maps section drawing field frames to a canvas with a sequential ramp, a time
  slider and a hover readout, built by a subagent; see the addendum below for what was
  verified by eye.

## Honest limits

- The clear-sky model is a placeholder with two parameters (transmittance 0.7, diffuse
  fraction 0.1). It will be replaced by a model with a published validation when there
  is a radiation record to score against; ERA5 surface solar radiation at M4 is the
  first candidate, a pyranometer station the better one.
- No sky-view factor, no elevation correction, no refraction, no eclipses yet (the
  calendar has the 2027 eclipse; the sun module does not read the calendar until V4).
- The ledger closing here is partly self-referential: the same code computes the flows
  and the declared stock. It proves the plumbing and units, not the physics. M2 gives
  the ledger two modules to disagree with each other.
- Single-threaded; 19.6 s per year at 20k cells. Fine for M1, and the hot loop can be
  parallelised per row when a rung needs it.

## Addendum: viewer verified

Screenshots of the M1 June week and the M2 year were read by the agent in this session:
elevation, irradiance and slope maps render with the river network visible, legends and
slider work, the ledger strip shows 365 green squares for the M2 year with leaks of
-152 J and 65 J against a 1 MJ tolerance. The M1 runs were regenerated after the
pixel-is-point fix; their digests changed (the field data moved by half a source pixel),
their physics did not.
