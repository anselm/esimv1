# 2026-11-01 — The 2100 scenario: what exists, what it would take to make it visceral

Anselm asked whether the P1 hindcast is "the 2100 scenario" or whether a new project
should capture it, and then set out the real question: could a lay person look at one of
these runs and appreciate that by 2100 we may be in serious trouble, and what would make
that visceral. Agent: Claude (Fable 5.1), earth-simulator-1 room on canada1.

## Two new projects, both assumptions and labelled so

The P1 run ends where the record ends. Past 2024 a planetary run stops being a hindcast,
and the emissions module now takes that as a named parameter, `after_record`: hold the
2024 rate, stop, or a straight line to zero in a given year. The manifest must say which
and why. Two projects:

- `anselm/planet-co2-1850-2100`: emissions held at the 2024 rate to 2100. "What if
  nothing changes."
- `anselm/planet-co2-zero-2050`: a straight line from the 2024 rate to zero in 2050.
  "What if the fastest plausible turn happened."

ppm and °C above 1850. Both ledgers close 3,044 times; each run takes under two seconds.

Read with the P1 caveats, which are not small: this carbon cycle keeps too much CO2
airborne (478 against the measured 425 in 2024), the only forcing is CO2, and nothing
feeds back. So the held case is a CO2-only sketch that starts 53 ppm high, and the
numbers are not to be quoted as projections. What the pair shows honestly is the shape:
under held emissions the warming does not level off; under a hard stop the surface
plateaus near its 2040 value and barely falls by 2100, while the deep ocean goes on
warming for the whole century and the planet is still taking in half a watt per square
metre in 2099. That is the lag in the pasted text, with a ledger behind it. The surface
stops; the commitment does not.

## Is this the 2100 scenario?

It is the bottom two layers of it, and it is not visceral. A lay person sees a number
going up. The pasted conversation's force comes from layers 3 to 7: where the heat goes,
what it does to water, crops and bodies, and what that does to people and the places they
live. None of that exists here yet, and the honest way to build it is the same as for the
basin: one module per layer, each with its own record and its own stated response, each
saying what it leaves out, and an auditor that measures rather than narrates.

The order to build: methane and N2O (a morning); the Berkeley Earth pattern layer and a
world map in the viewer (a day); WorldPop, Natural Earth and the UN totals, and the
exposure count (a day); the tipping-element table below; mortality last, because it is
the one most easily abused.

## Moving maps

The maps are not static; the September fire's burn, the deer, the soil and the leaf area
all move under the cursor. The planet runs show no maps because nothing in them is
gridded. The pattern layer changes that: three world maps under the same cursor as the
charts, the anomaly, the absolute summer temperature, and the people under a threshold.
The viewer needs two things for a world grid: coastlines drawn over the field (Natural
Earth, 110 m, 215 KB), and a pin. The pin is the visceral piece: a person types or
allows their place, and the page shows that one cell's summer, year by year, against
the summer of a year they remember. Everything else on the page is context for that
chart.

## Scoring: counting people

Deaths are the number the pasted text reaches for, and the honest version is in two
parts. Exposure is a count, not a model: people living in cells that cross a threshold,
by country, by year, from a population layer and a temperature layer whose provenance
is stated. Mortality is a model, with published coefficients and wide bands, and the
page must show the band and the paper. The auditor reports both as it reports any stock:
how many in 2024, how many at the cursor, the year each country's count crosses a level.
A manifest without a population layer gets no such count and says so, which is the
"not every scenario has every metric" rule working as designed.

## Tearing points

Country by country stress is where the words have outrun the rigour, including in the
pasted text, which is careful to say so. Three honest things can be done at this scale:

1. **Thresholds with provenance.** The tipping-elements assessment (Armstrong McKay et
   al. 2022, Science; recalled, to be fetched) gives each element a central estimate and
   a range of global warming at which it is likely to tip, and a timescale. That table
   belongs on the wiki and in `data/events/`, and an auditor reads it: when the run's
   global mean crosses an element's range, the audit names the element, the range and
   the paper. That is a tear signal at layers 3 to 5 that a reader can check.
2. **Precedents instead of indices.** For heat, the records hold events people remember
   and their tolls: 2003 in Europe, 2010 in Russia, 2015 in India and Pakistan, 2021 in
   the Pacific Northwest. A country's exposure in the run can be shown against the
   exposure of those years rather than against an invented stress index. "Your country's
   ordinary summer in 2060 is the summer of 2003" is checkable; a riot date is not.
3. **Recovery against recurrence**, the auditor's existing measure, applied to annual
   series per country once there is a stock to watch. What recovers after a heat season
   at the country scale is not something we observe yet, so this waits for a stock with
   a record behind it (harvests are the likely one).

What this project should refuse to do is produce a collapse date per country. The hormuz
room had a country stress view and Anselm says it clouded over; it clouded because the
stressors had no sources. Here each stressor is a module with a record, or it is absent.

## What makes it visceral for a lay person

In order of what I believe matters, with the admission that this is design judgement and
not something measured:

1. **Their place.** One cell, one chart, year by year. Everything global is abstract
   until it is their July.
2. **A year they remember** as the anchor on that chart. 2003, 2021, or last summer.
3. **One count that moves** with the cursor: people living where the body cannot work
   outside in summer. Not a temperature.
4. **The two scenarios side by side** under one cursor: held and zero by 2050. The gap
   between them is the decision; the deep-ocean line that rises in both is the part
   already bought.
5. **The lag made plain**: when the cursor passes 2050 in the zero case and the surface
   stops but the ocean does not, that is the sentence from the pasted text drawn.

A "2100" page in the viewer that pairs the two projects under one cursor is a small
addition to what exists and the right next viewer task after the world map.

## Measure of success

Show the pair to someone who does not read climate papers, with a pin on their town.
Ask them what they would tell a friend. If the answer is a number, we failed; if it is
"my summers" or "the ocean keeps going", we did not.
