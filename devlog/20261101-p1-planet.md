# 2026-11-01 — P1 Planet: the bottom of the cascade in one box

The first far-field rung, built the same afternoon as `20261101-far-field.md`. Agent:
Claude (Fable 5.1), earth-simulator-1 room on canada1. Anselm's ask: "I want to try at
least build a model that can demonstrate that. Right now I am very interested in trying
to understand knock on effects of co2 rise."

## What it is

Zero-dimensional. Four module kinds in `crates/modules/src/planet.rs`, one manifest,
`manifests/p1-planet-co2-1850-2025.toml`, 175 years in monthly steps:

- `carbon.emissions`: the Global Carbon Project's record (through Our World in Data's
  `owid-co2-data.csv`, World rows) of fossil and land-use-change CO2, converted to GtC and
  released into the atmosphere account of a carbon ledger, month by month. Nothing is
  modelled here; this is what the record says was emitted.
- `carbon.pools`: the airborne fraction by impulse response. Each tonne emitted splits
  into four pools: one that stays, three that decay to a `sinks` account on timescales
  of 394, 37 and 4.3 years (Joos et al. 2013, the multi-model response used for IPCC
  AR5's metrics; the fractions and timescales are recalled from memory and the describe
  says so). Reports ppm.
- `energy.twolayer`: forcing 5.35 ln(C/C0) from the ppm, a surface layer and a deep ocean
  (Held et al. 2010; Geoffroy et al. 2013 multi-model mean parameters, same caveat),
  integrated as an energy ledger in joules: forcing in from space, response out to
  space, uptake into the deep. CO2 only; the other gases and the aerosols are absent.
- `obs.planet`: the records as series, reported each 1 July: Law Dome ice-core CO2 until
  Mauna Loa begins in 1959, Mauna Loa annual means after; HadCRUT5 and GISTEMP global
  temperature, each re-zeroed over 1850 to 1900.

Five new catalog entries carry the sources, what was verified today and the licence
notes still to be fetched. NOAA's ocean heat content server answered 503 all afternoon,
so there is no ocean heat scoreboard yet; the module reports stored energy in ZJ ready
for one.

Nothing was tuned to the curves it is scored against. The parameters are the published
ones and the start concentration is the ice core's 1850 value.

## What it does

Both ledgers close 2,131 times with worst leaks of 4e-12 GtC and 3e8 J (on stocks of
1e24 J). The kernel's time is 14 ms; the second it takes to run is reading a 14 MB CSV.

## What it says, honestly

**The shape is right and the carbon sink is too weak.** Cumulative emissions since 1850
come to 745 GtC by mid-2024 in this record; the observed rise of 138 ppm is 293 GtC, an
airborne fraction of 0.39. The impulse response keeps 0.55 airborne, so the model ends at
478 ppm instead of 425. That is a known property of using a pulse response derived on a
present-day background (Joos's PD100 case) for the whole industrial period: the early
sinks, working against a small excess, were relatively stronger than the response
function allows. The fix by the house rules is not to move a fraction by hand but to
replace the response function with boxes that have their own stocks (a mixed layer with
carbonate chemistry, a deep ocean, land with a fertilisation response) and to let the
hindcast harness move their named parameters against this scoreboard. That is M12's
job, and p1 is now its second customer after the Boone hydrology.

**The temperature match is partly a coincidence and the table shows where.** By 2024 the
model is within 0.06 °C of HadCRUT5, but with too much CO2 (2.74 W/m² of forcing against
roughly 2.2 for the real CO2 concentration) standing in for the non-CO2 gases and the
aerosols it does not have. In 1950 the model is 0.33 °C too warm, which is the
mid-century aerosol cooling it cannot represent, on top of the CO2 excess. A run with
the right CO2 and no aerosols would come in low; one with both would come in close for
the right reasons. The next entries to this layer are therefore methane, N2O and an
aerosol forcing series, each with its own record, not a better fit.

**The ledger makes the argument the pasted denialist text gets wrong.** The carbon
ledger's `atmosphere` stock is the excess mass. Molecules turn over against the sinks in
years (the 4.3-year pool is exactly the fast exchange the bomb-carbon curve measures);
the excess drains on the slower timescales and a fifth of it, in this response function,
not at all on any timescale here. Both facts are in one ledger that closes. The
distinction between turnover and excess is not a matter of interpretation once the
accounts are kept.

**The lag is visible.** The deep ocean anomaly is 0.52 °C in 2024 against 1.60 at the
surface and the planet is taking in 0.93 W/m² more than it emits. That imbalance is the
commitment the far-field entry talks about: it is heat already in the system that has
not yet shown at the surface. Observed estimates of the present imbalance are of the
same order (around 1 W/m² in recent years, from CERES, recalled and not fetched).

## What the viewer showed

The run page renders: both ledgers, three scores, the paired chart of modelled against
observed temperature, the airborne fraction falling from 1 towards 0.55, the deep-ocean
curve. Two things are wrong with it for a run this long: 2,131 ledger closes drawn as
squares take most of the page, and a 175-year series should be able to show the obs on
the CO2 chart as it does on the temperature one (it does, further down). The close grid
needs to collapse to a line when there are more closes than fit.
