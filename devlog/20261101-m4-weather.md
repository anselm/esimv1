# 2026-11-01 — M4 Weather from outside: ERA5 drives the basin, and scores the sun

Overnight, same session as M3 (Claude, Fable 5.1, canada1).

## Source

Open-Meteo's archive API serves hourly ERA5 reanalysis for any point from 1940 with about
a five-day lag, anonymously, free for non-commercial use (a paid service would need their
commercial tier; recorded in the catalog). Checked from canada1: on 21 June 2025 at the
basin centre ERA5's shortwave peaks at 935 W/m2 at 19Z; our clear-sky peaks at 948 W/m2 in
the same hour. Its ensemble API serves 31 GEFS members for forecasts, which is the route
to branches later. ERA5-Land through the same API lacks shortwave; plain ERA5 is used.

## What runs

`weather.era5` fetches one year of hourly temperature, precipitation, shortwave, humidity
and wind for a 0.25-degree grid of points over the window (12 points here) in one request
per year, cached and hash-locked like everything else, interpolates bilinearly to the run
grid, and publishes five shared fields every hour at priority -1 so the sun and the basin
see the current hour. `water.basin` gained `forcing = "wx"`: per-cell hourly rain and air
temperature from those fields instead of the one station. Year 2025 at 15 arc-seconds:
60 s, both ledgers close 365 times (water worst leak 5e-6 m3).

## Two scores, two honest findings

Clear-sky sun against ERA5 all-sky shortwave, 8,760 hours:

| NSE | KGE | r | bias | mean clear-sky | mean ERA5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.676 | 0.689 | 0.885 | +23.7% | 219 W/m2 | 177 W/m2 |

The annual clearness index (ERA5 over clear-sky) is 0.74 to 0.85 from April to December,
which is the cloud. In January and February it is 1.01 and 0.96: ERA5's all-sky exceeds
our clear-sky, which is impossible if the clear-sky model were right. The Meinel formula
is too pessimistic at low sun (large air mass), and snow-covered ground adds multiple
reflection that no clear-sky model without albedo sees. Finding: the placeholder clear-sky
model under-reads in winter by at least a few percent; replace it (REST2 or Ineichen with
a turbidity climatology) before any energy-balance result is trusted in winter.

Discharge against USGS, with ERA5 rain instead of the Webster City gauge:

| forcing | NSE | KGE | r | bias | peak sim / obs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| station (M3) | 0.469 | 0.435 | 0.736 | +31% | 149 / 166 m3/s |
| ERA5 gridded (M4) | 0.254 | 0.383 | 0.556 | +27% | 274 / 166 m3/s |

Worse, not better. ERA5's annual total is close (1,023 mm against the station's 1,090), but
its summer timing is not: June comes out drier than the river shows and August produces
a flood the river did not have (sim 62 m3/s monthly mean against 34, peak 274 against 166).
A 31-kilometre reanalysis is not a rain gauge for convective summer rain in Iowa; this is
well known in the hydrology literature and the harness just reproduced it in an hour.
Finding: for hindcasts use a gauge-based gridded precipitation product (the candidates the
agent knows of are PRISM, Daymet and NOAA's Stage IV; none checked yet); keep ERA5 for
temperature, radiation and humidity; keep the station as a check. The winter and autumn
wet bias is unchanged, which confirms it is the soil model's, not the rain's.

## What this rung proves

Weather enters only through the registry and the world store; no module knows where it
came from. The sun got a scoreboard and failed part of it, which is the scoreboard working.
Two forcings were compared by changing one manifest line. That is the ensemble mechanism
in miniature: branches are the same manifest with a different member.
