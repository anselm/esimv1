## What runs

`manifests/m3-water-boone.toml`: the sun module (hourly, 15 arc-seconds) and a new
`water.basin` module over a window 42.3 to 43.2 N, 94.2 to 93.5 W. The module:

- delineates the basin above the gauge from the DEM alone: priority-flood depression
  filling (Barnes 2014), D8 flow direction, accumulation, pour point snapped to the
  highest accumulation within three cells of the gauge. Result: 2,172 km2 at 15" (2,186
  km2 at a two-kilometre snap in the standalone check) against USGS's 844 sq mi, 2,186 km2.
  No synthetic terrain anywhere; the Boone River's network emerges from the Copernicus
  tiles.
- takes rain, snowfall and temperature from GHCN-Daily station USC00138806 (Webster
  City, 42.4686 N 93.7975 W, record from 1893), applied uniformly over the basin, each
  day's total spread over the 24 hours ending at the 0700 local observation.
- runs a degree-day snowpack, a single soil bucket with saturation excess and linear
  drainage, evaporation limited by the sun module's energy field (read from the shared
  world store) and scaled by soil wetness, and linear-reservoir routing down the D8
  network at two velocities (channel above 2 km2 of upstream area, hillslope below).
- closes a water ledger in cubic metres every day across atmosphere, snowpack, soil,
  channel, evaporation and outlet. Year 2025: 365 closes, worst leak 7e-6 m3 on flows of
  2.4e9 m3. The energy ledger from M1 closes beside it.
- reports simulated and observed daily mean discharge on local days, so `esim score`
  can compare them.

Runtime 40 s for the year (37 s of it the sun at 15" over 36k cells; the water module
over 13.9k basin cells is cheap). `esim verify` on the June manifest: same digest twice,
exact replay from a snapshot.

## The score

`esim score` is new: Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency, Kling-Gupta efficiency, correlation,
percent bias and RMSE between any two series of a run at matching times, written to
`scores.json` in the run folder.

Against USGS 05481000 daily discharge for 2025, 365 days, default parameters, no
calibration:

| | sim | obs |
|---|---|---|
| NSE | 0.469 | |
| KGE | 0.435 | |
| r | 0.736 | |
| bias | +31.4% | |
| mean, m3/s | 32.3 | 24.6 |
| peak, m3/s | 149 | 166 |

Monthly means tell the story: the summer flood is in the right place and roughly the right
size (June 47 vs 68, July 74 vs 83 m3/s), and winter and autumn are far too wet (January
31 vs 0.8, October 26 vs 4.6). The model has no frozen ground, its drainage is a constant
fraction, and its soil starts half full. Two quick variants confirmed the trade: slower
drainage with a bigger bucket removes the bias (-3.5%) but flattens the peaks (NSE 0.33);
faster drainage sharpens peaks and worsens the bias (+46%, NSE 0.38). The structure, not
the parameters, is what limits it, which is what a scoreboard is for. No calibration was
attempted beyond those two runs; that is M11 and M12 work, done by method, not by hand.

For context the agent is confident of: NSE above 0.5 is the conventional threshold for a
"satisfactory" monthly hydrological model in the applied literature, and daily NSE above
0.7 is good. Reaching 0.47 on day one with one station and two parameters says the
plumbing is right and the physics is a sketch. Both were the goal.

## Other changes

- Registry: readers for USGS NWIS daily values (RDB) and GHCN-Daily station CSV, with
  catalog entries recording what was verified and the provisional status of recent USGS
  data. Flow network and basin mask are cached derived layers like slope and horizon.
- Run ids now include a hash of the manifest text as well as the log digest, because two
  runs with different parameters produced the same event log (parameters that change
  only ledger and series values are invisible to the log) and would have collided.
- An integration test runs June 2025 and asserts that both ledgers close, the delineated
  area is within 5% of USGS, and observed discharge loads.

## Honest limits

One rain gauge for 2,000 km2 is the largest single error source in summer; gridded
precipitation (M4) is the fix. No frozen soil, no tile-drain representation beyond the
linear term, no channel geometry, no lakes, no abstractions. The pour point is two
kilometres below the gauge. USGS 2025 data are provisional. GHCN's 2025 record at this
station is missing three precipitation days, treated as zero.
