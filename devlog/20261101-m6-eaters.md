# 2026-11-01 — M6 Eaters: cohorts in cells, a population ledger, spatial events in the kernel

Overnight, same session (Claude, Fable 5.1, canada1).

## Kernel: spatial events

Events gained an optional cell (`at`). A module can publish an event about a cell
(`publish_at`), send one to a named module about a cell, and subscribe to a topic only for
a set of cells (`subscribe_at`). The kernel keeps a topic-to-cell-to-module index, so a
cell-addressed event reaches the topic's subscribers plus the modules subscribed at that
cell, deduplicated, in deterministic order. The index is in snapshots so replay is exact.
This is orbital's spatial hash inside the kernel, as the design entry said it would be.

A decision made while building M6: bulk per-cell numbers travel in shared fields, and
events only signal that a field is ready. A grazing request for 13,900 cells is one event
and one field, not 13,900 events. Cell-addressed events are for sparse things: an ignition,
a dam failure, a sale of one parcel. M7 uses them that way first.

## What runs

`manifests/m6-eaters-boone.toml`: weather, sun, water, crop, and two new modules,
`fauna.herbivore` (a deer-sized grazer, 6 per km2 at the start) and `fauna.predator` (a
coyote-sized predator, 0.3 per km2), each one cohort per basin cell in the manner of
Madingley's functional groups. Daily, in priority order at the day boundary:

1. the herbivore writes its demand per cell and publishes `graze.request`;
2. the crop removes up to 30% of standing biomass per cell, transfers carbon from
   `biomass` to `herbivores`, writes `veg.grazed_kgc` and publishes `graze.done`;
3. the herbivore eats (plus a baseline of browse and residue from an untracked `browse`
   account, because Iowa deer do not live on standing corn), assimilates half, respires,
   gives birth when fed, dies at a background rate plus starvation, and moves a tenth of
   each cell's count to the richest neighbour;
4. the predator computes kills per cell by a Holling type II response on the herbivore
   density it reads from `fauna.herb_n`, writes `fauna.pred_kills` and publishes
   `predation.request`;
5. the herbivore removes the prey, moves their body carbon to `predators`, writes
   `fauna.prey_taken`, publishes `predation.done`;
6. the predator assimilates, breeds, dies, and moves toward prey.

Two ledgers through the animals. `animals`, in individuals: `births` into each cohort,
out to `deaths_natural` and `deaths_predation`. Movement between cells is internal and
exact, so the declared count matches the flows to 3e-10 individuals over the year. The
`carbon` ledger now runs atmosphere to crop to herbivore to predator and back out through
respiration, dung and carcasses, closing at 1.3e-5 kgC. Four ledgers close together
(animals, carbon, energy, water); the year takes 17 s.

## The numbers, honestly

| month | deer | coyotes | grazed kgC/day | killed/day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | 11,455 | 645 | 0 | 22 |
| June | 6,098 | 742 | 3,045 | 21 |
| September | 4,800 | 797 | 2,397 | 21 |
| December | 2,458 | 807 | 0 | 19 |

The deer decline through the year and the coyotes rise. That is the parameters, not the
mechanism: the starvation term is heavy against a browse baseline of 0.2 kgC per animal
per day, the birth rate is low, and nothing here is calibrated. Iowa's real deer herd is
roughly stable under hunting, which is not modelled at all. There is no scoreboard for
this rung yet (state deer surveys exist; none fetched). What the rung proves is the
mechanism: two cohort modules and a plant module negotiate carbon and individuals through
events and shared fields, counts are conserved exactly through movement, and the whole
chain replays bitwise from a snapshot, which the new integration test checks on June.
