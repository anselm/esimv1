# 2026-11-03 — The slope: why Rothermel climbs the canyons and our engine did not

Anselm, late in the evening, looking at the two arrival maps: "It's funny how the older model is
now doing a much better job of crawling up valleys and our new model is still looking like it is
not sensitive to terrain." He is right, and the reason is in two lines of code.

## Diagnosis

Rothermel's spread rate is R = R0 (1 + φ_w + φ_s) with the slope factor φ_s = 5.275 β^-0.3 tan²φ
(`wildfire.rs`, recalled from Rothermel 1972 and not yet checked against the paper). For a shrub
bed with packing ratio β near 0.01 (a typical value, recalled) the coefficient is about 20, so a
30° slope multiplies the no-wind rate by about 7 and a 20° slope by about 3.6. The wind it reads is
the ERA5 point at 25 km, 7 m/s on the first afternoon, through a wind adjustment factor, so the
slope term is of the same order as the wind term and the fire follows the ground: up Topanga's
side canyons, along the ridges north of the ignition, in fingers.

Our engine put slope only in the e-folding length of the heat a burning cell delivers:
λ = λ0 (1 + 1.5 tan φ cos) with λ0 = 20 m, and the heat q = I exp(−d/λ) at d ≈ 30 m. On the flat
that is 0.22 I; straight uphill at 30° it is 0.45 I, twice the flat rate where Rothermel has eight
times. Linear in tan φ where Rothermel is quadratic, and capped: exp(−d/λ) can never pass 1, so no
direction, however steep or windy, can deliver more than 4.5 times the flat heat. Meanwhile the
HRRR wind at 10 to 20 m/s with `wind_gain` 0.15 gives 2.5 to 4 times, so the wind term had the
cap nearly to itself and the slope hardly entered. Downhill was the same as flat. With the near
reach (only the next cell is heated) the time to ignite is threshold over q, and q varied by a
factor of two across the compass, so the arrival map came out as concentric rings stretched by the
wind. A drop on fabric, as he said this morning.

## What was built

One new knob, `slope_rate_gain`, which puts Rothermel's shape on the heat delivered rather than on
its reach: uphill q is multiplied by 1 + g tan²φ cos, downhill divided by 1 + g' tan²φ |cos|,
with g' = `downslope_gain` (defaults to g; the downhill half is a guess, since Rothermel's backing
rate is the ellipse's and not a division). At 0, the default, the engine is unchanged and every
earlier run reproduces. Three variants on the near-4 setting:

| variant | uphill g | downhill g' | 27 h acres (mapped 15,824) | end acres (mapped 23,343) | Sørensen | structures recall | precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| near-4 (before) | 0 | 0 | 15,306 | 46,759 | 0.59 | 0.12 | 0.40 |
| near-4-slope | 20 | 20 | 1,117 | 2,725 | 0.20 | 0.01 | 0.41 |
| near-4-slope-6 | 6 | 6 | 7,015 | 31,062 | 0.70 | 0.10 | 0.43 |
| near-4-slope-soft | 20 | 2 | 24,186 | 52,745 | 0.55 | 0.16 | 0.44 |

Rothermel's own gain with the symmetric division (near-4-slope) runs up the one canyon north of the
ignition and stops: from a ridge top every direction is downhill, the heat is divided by seven, and
with the cell cooling between doses nothing below ever crosses its threshold. The same cliff as this
morning's, from the other side. The same gain with a gentle downhill (near-4-slope-soft) overruns
the first day by half as much again and the record by the end. The middle setting, gain 6 both
ways, has the best overlap of the mapped perimeter of any run on either engine so far (0.70 against
0.59 for near-4 and 0.58 for Rothermel's reanalysis), and its arrival map has the fingers: the fire
climbs toward Topanga and along the ridges north instead of filling a disc. It is half the record's
area at 27 hours, which the ignition multiplier can be lowered to meet, and ends a third over.

## What it means

Terrain sensitivity was not missing from the data, it was missing from the formula, and the form
of the formula matters more than the constants. The exponential-in-reach form caps every
directional effect at 1/exp(−d/λ0); a multiplicative rate factor does not. The next thing to try is
to drop λ as the carrier of direction altogether and write the heat as I·f(d)·(1 + φ_w + φ_s) with
φ_w from the wind in Rothermel's shape too, so wind and slope add as vectors as they do in the
older model, and the ellipse's backing rate replaces the downhill division. That is a morning's
work and should be done before terrain-following wind, since the wind module will only feed a
direction into the same formula.
