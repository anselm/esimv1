# 2026-11-01 — Far field: projects over runs, scoring and tearing, the planetary stack

Anselm looked at the viewer and the repo after the first push and wrote a long message.
This entry is the assessment it asked for; `20261101-p1-planet.md` is the first rung it
led to. Agent: Claude (Fable 5.1), earth-simulator-1 room on canada1.

## What he said, in short

The viewer is "reasonable for a raw data view, a good start". The meta objective holds:
a big list of granular tools people mix and match, not a product, because he does not
want peers to feel he is competing with them. Then five directions:

1. **Projects foremost.** A user conversationally specifies a manifest; that is their
   project. Weak users and areas by path (`/anselm/abiotic-models/irradiance-example-1`)
   with no accounts; a person picks a path, describes the project to an LLM (there is an
   Anthropic key), a manifest is generated and run. This implies a server side that
   accepts a parameterised request to create a project. The home page becomes a list of
   projects or manifests with a plus button; raw runs belong inside a project.
2. **An interactive view of a run**: watch the system evolve, scrub a timeline, share by
   URL. Charts of the things he cares about (predators and prey over time). Raw events
   somewhere, not in front.
3. **Spatial extent** as part of creating a manifest, probably in the conversation;
   initial placement of agents.
4. **A ladder of manifests** from trivial to reasonably complex, exercising modular runs.
5. **Scoring and tearing.** Intangibles: quality of life, resilience of a bioregion to
   shock (diversity as resilience), an ecosystem being spent down or improving. A scoring
   module as an independent auditor; metrics apply only when their ingredients exist.
   Tearing: we have no concept of how a system actually fails (all prey gone; too hot for
   predators, which the predator model does not consider). "Two hard things."
6. **Today: far field.** Planetary phenomena matter to the most people. Build a model
   that demonstrates the knock-on effects of CO2 rise, real consequences, cascades, real
   timelines, stretching versus tearing. He pasted a conversation with a layered stack:
   solar input; atmospheric composition; energy balance and where the heat goes (the
   ocean, ice); abiotic earth systems; biogeochemistry and its feedbacks (where tipping
   points live); primary producers; consumers and food webs; human systems with their own
   internal cascade; and the cross-layer feedbacks and the 20 to 40 year lag between the
   lower layers and the top.

## Assessment

### Projects, areas, paths

Nothing in the kernel resists this; it is a storage and routing question and the pieces
are already decided. A project is a folder: `manifest.toml`, `README.md` (the
conversation's summary of what the person wanted, in their words), `runs/`. Its path is
its identity: `<area>/<name>` under one root, with `area` being a person's chosen word
today and an account later. The viewer's home becomes a list of projects read from the
root; a project page shows its manifest, its description, and its runs; a run page is
what the viewer shows today, minus the raw events, which move behind a tab. Nothing about
this needs user accounts; it needs a server that can write a folder and start a job. On
the decided infrastructure that is one small service: accept a path and a description,
call the model with `esim kinds --json` as its tool surface, write the manifest, run
`esim check`, store the folder in R2, start a Cloud Run job, write the run back. The
facade is the one piece of this that is new; everything it calls exists.

One caution from the roots entry still applies: the project is the manifest and its
runs, not a thing the viewer owns. Anyone must be able to take the folder and run it
with the CLI, which is what keeps this a bucket of tools rather than a product.

### Timeline scrub

The run file already has what a scrubber needs: every frame and every series point is
stamped with the kernel's time. What the viewer lacks is one time cursor shared by maps
and charts. That is a viewer change, not a kernel change. Runs that are long in years
and fine in steps will need the writer to thin frames for the viewer, which the frame
cadence parameter already allows.

### Spatial extent and initial placement

The bounding box is repeated per module in every manifest; "place once" has been pending
since the roots entry and this makes it due. A `[place]` table at the top of the manifest,
inherited by modules that do not override it, and in the conversation the LLM asks for a
place and resolves it to a box. Initial agent placement belongs to the module that owns
the agents, from a real layer where one exists (land cover, census, a gauge network) and
from a stated rule where none does; the manifest carries the rule, the describe states it.

### The ladder

`manifests/` is already a ladder (m0 buckets to m11 nitrogen, f1 forecast, now p1
planet) but it reads as a build log, not as a curriculum. A `scenarios/` folder with a
README per rung, in the order a person should meet them, is still the right move; it was
listed in the roots entry and is not done.

### Scoring and tearing

An auditor is a module like any other: it reads fields and subscribes to series, holds no
stocks, reports scores. The describe verb makes "not every simulation has every metric"
mechanical: an auditor declares its reads as optional, and `esim check` reports which
metrics will be silent in this manifest and why ("cost_of_living needs an economy
module"). Three auditors suggest themselves from his list:

- **Diversity** as resilience: an evenness index over whatever populations exist
  (cohort fields, crop kinds). Cheap and honest.
- **Spend-down**: for every ledger with a declared stock, the trend of the stock against
  its inflow. A basin whose soil organic nitrogen falls every year is being spent; a
  population whose births fall short of deaths is being spent. The ledgers already hold
  this; nobody reads it as a score yet.
- **Recovery versus recurrence**, which is his own definition of tearing from the pasted
  text: measure how long a series takes to return to its pre-shock band after each
  shock, and compare with the interval between shocks. Stretching is recovery shorter
  than recurrence; tearing is the reverse. The M7 hazard work gives the shocks; the
  auditor gives the verdict. This is the one tearing metric that does not need a theory
  of the system, only its record.

What tearing means inside a module is the harder half: a state the module declares as
not recoverable (a cohort below its minimum viable number, a crop past its lethal
temperature, a channel that has cut a new course). Today the predator model has no
temperature at all. The honest way in is to give modules named thresholds with stated
provenance and let the auditor record when they are crossed; where no provenance exists
the threshold is a guess and says so.

### The planetary stack

His stack is the kernel's natural layering. Mapped onto what exists and what could:

| layer | status |
|---|---|
| 0 solar | `sun.irradiance` exists for a place; globally a constant on these timescales |
| 1 atmospheric composition | **p1**: observed emissions into a carbon ledger, impulse-response sinks; methane, N2O, aerosols are more of the same pattern with their own records |
| 2 energy balance, where the heat goes | **p1**: a two-layer balance with an energy ledger; ice as a phase-change account is the next entry |
| 3 abiotic earth systems | sea level from thermal expansion plus ice is a box model with records to score against; weather and circulation are not ours to simulate, they are data (reanalysis, model archives) we read |
| 4 biogeochemical feedbacks | box models with published parameters (permafrost, ocean uptake decline, fire carbon); this is where the tipping-element literature gives thresholds with ranges, and where the tearing auditor gets its planetary table |
| 5 primary producers | `veg.crop` is a start for a place; globally, a sink response to CO2 and temperature with the carbon budget's own land-sink record as the scoreboard |
| 6 consumers | the cohort modules exist for a basin; a planetary version is where honesty gets hard |
| 7 human systems | agents exist for a basin (farms, rules); the economy, health and governance cascade in the pasted text is where speculation lives, and where the project's rule "integrate, do not speculate" means we stop or declare guesses loudly |

The lesson of the pasted text for the design is the lag: the lower layers commit decades
before the upper layers feel it. A model that only shows layers 1 and 2 already shows the
commitment, with ledgers, from real emissions. That is where p1 starts, and it is enough
to demonstrate the mechanism the denialist text in the paste gets wrong: a carbon ledger
distinguishes a molecule's turnover from the excess mass, because the stock the ledger
checks is the excess.
