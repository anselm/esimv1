Anselm sent six links late on 2026-11-03 ("I see a few links"). What each is, what was readable
from here, and what it gives the fire work. Everything below was fetched today unless marked.

## 2. fsri.org, the Southern California Fires Incident Analysis Report

UL Research Institutes' Fire Safety Research Institute, at the request of the Office of the
Governor of California. Two reports:

- Phase One, *Southern California Fires Timeline Report*, published 2025-11-20, DOI
  10.60752/102376.29905157 (figshare; the PDF is 34 MB and figshare was still preparing the
  download when this was written, HTTP 202).
- Phase Two, *Southern California Fires Incident Analysis Report*, published 2026-10-02, DOI
  10.60752/102376.31982232, CC BY-SA 4.0, nine authors (Alkonis, Gorham, Horn, Willi, Kerber,
  Schrader, Knight, Hodges, Harned), 82 MB PDF, "175 findings and 266 recommendations".

The thing that matters for us is the **timestamped dataset** the page links (`s.ul.org/fsri-califonia-data-p2`,
a public Google Sheet, readable anonymously as CSV through the `gviz` export). Five sheets:

| sheet (gid) | rows | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1961773423 | 4,785 | Palisades events, 2025-01-02 to 01-09, almost all on 01-07 (3,777) and 01-08 (996) |
| 1341829632 | about 9,000 lines | Eaton events |
| 1125301104 | about 950 lines | the Lachman fire of 1 January, whose holdover is the suspected origin |
| 578253915 | about 5,100 lines | weather: NWS products, soundings, SCE and CWOP station reports |
| 1717589843 | 13 | the title sheet and the caveats |

Columns: source, date, time (local), incident, location, units, description. Sources for the
Palisades sheet are led by LAPD CAD (1,121 rows), LA County fire radio V-1 (615), LASD Lost Hills
(343), a Malibu weather station (342), LAFD incident details (185), county dispatch (151), LAFD
Tac-2 and Tac-5, the county EOC's 214 logs, the LAPD after-action report and SCE circuit reports.
UL's own caveat: radio was transcribed by machine and is incomplete; "should not be considered a
complete record of events".

What it gives us, counted by keyword in the Palisades descriptions: 578 rows on evacuation, 94 on
spot fires and embers, 65 on drops, tankers and retardant, 33 on dozers, 26 on helicopters, 33 on
hydrants and water pressure, 767 on wind. And a **reported-acreage series from the radio and the
press**, far finer than the perimeters: 10 acres at 10:43, 200 at 11:20, 772 at 12:12, 1,308 at
15:17, 2,921 at 18:50 on 7 January (local), 11,802 by 11:27 and 15,832 by 13:34 on 8 January.
Two consequences for the scoring already in the repository:

- The "hand-drawn triangle" perimeter carries a 06:00Z timestamp (22:00 local on the 7th). The
  press and Watch Duty rows give 2,921 acres from 18:50 local; CAL FIRE's own status reports
  (section 7 below) say 1,262 acres from 15:34 through 22:20 and 2,921 at 00:29 on the 8th. The
  NIFC stamp sits between the two sources, and the triangle's acreage is a figure, not a map, so
  the first day cannot be scored from perimeters at all. The UL report's hourly maps can.
- A reported-acreage series is a second scoreboard for the first day, which neither project has:
  an `obs.reports` module reading CAL FIRE's reports and this sheet, each figure with its source.

This dataset is also the suppression record Anselm asked for, in prose with times: which engines
were where, when the drops started and stopped (the winds grounded aircraft on the night of the
7th, per the county's after-action review below), where lines were attempted.

## 3. lacounty.gov/aar

Los Angeles County's page for its after-action reviews of the Eaton and Palisades fires, by the
McChrystal Group: the first (alert, notification and evacuation, September 2025, with a May 2026
addendum) and the second (recovery and repopulation, press release dated 2026-09-11, "26
substantive findings and 25 actionable recommendations"). Also two Eaton fire investigation
reports and a Citygate Associates report on Eaton evacuation alerts. The page states no GIS and
no figures beyond those; the first review's finding quoted on the page is that hurricane-force
winds "grounded all firefighting aircraft" and made "nighttime aerial surveillance impossible on
the critical night of January 7". Prose for the suppression module's assumptions; the PDFs were
not read today.

## 4. FEMA GIS hub, "Incident Page - California Wildfires January 2025"

A JavaScript page that renders nothing to a plain fetch; its content is the ArcGIS Hub item
`1ee8ccb97b8d44a7ae2ae0d23e90ee26`, whose data is public and was read instead. Declaration
DR-4856-CA, incident period 7 to 31 January 2025. What it points at that is public:

- **NOAA NGS emergency response imagery** (`storms.ngs.noaa.gov/storms/2025_eri`): XYZ tile
  services, anonymous, with bounds in the index. Over our window: Maxar scenes for 2025-01-09,
  01-10, 01-13, 01-14, 01-16, 01-18, 01-20 (zoom to 19) and NOAA's own aerial RGB of 2025-01-28
  (-118.76 to -117.97, 34.01 to 34.23, zoom to 21). This is the before-and-after imagery for the
  structure record, at a resolution Sentinel-2 cannot give; the 01-09 Maxar scene is during the fire.
- CAL FIRE DINS public views for Palisades and Eaton (already in the catalog).
- NASA Disasters mapping portal (fire points, perimeters, Sentinel-1 change detection), USGS HDDS
  Explorer (ordering), the WFIGS perimeter services, the state's evacuation-zone aggregation.
- Not public: Nearmap and WorldView-2/3 imagery, an ICEYE building damage assessment, FireGuard
  (NIFC account). Noted so nobody goes looking.

## 5. ororatech.com, "The Palisades Fire visualized"

A scrollytelling piece by a wildfire-monitoring company (Fire Spread, Burnt Area and land surface
temperature products; insurance among its sectors). Data: their own and public low-Earth-orbit
hotspot detections, "5,128 hotspots from January 7–12, a peak of 2,482 on January 8"; a Sentinel-2
image of 7 January at 10:45 local; burnt area at 20 m two to three days after. No download or API
stated for this story. A neighbour in the sense of the ageospatial entry: retrieval and
visualisation of the record, not simulation. The public hotspot detections they draw on (VIIRS,
MODIS; recalled) are the thing to add to our record, as a third scoreboard: where heat was seen
from orbit each pass.

## 6. malibucity.org, Palisades Fire Incident Information Archive

The City of Malibu's archive of incident updates, 7 January to 12 February, mostly as linked PDFs.
In the page text: the 18:00 statement on the 7th (Santa Ana winds 50 to 80 mph, gusts "up to 100
mph", PCH closures, SCE shutoffs by circuit), the 23:30 update ("estimated at 2,921 acres",
evacuation zones MAL-C111-A/B, RMB-U030-A/B, MAL-C112-B), and the final status: "23,707 acres,
100% containment", 6,831 destroyed and 973 damaged across Palisades and Malibu by CAL FIRE's
count, 720 in the city. The evacuation zone ids are the Genasys zones, which the state's
evacuation-zone service carries; the time an order was issued per zone is the evacuation
module's input when it comes.

## 7. fire.ca.gov, the incident page ("this may be authoritative")

Sent an hour later. The incident page: start 01/07/2025 10:30 AM, "Southeast of Palisades Drive,
Pacific Palisades", 23,448 acres, 100% contained 01/31/2025 10:48 AM, cause "Arson" (the page was
last updated 04/09/2026; the status reports of January 2025 say "Under Investigation"), 6,845
structures destroyed, 975 damaged, 12 civilian fatalities, agency having jurisdiction the Los
Angeles City Fire Department, "Not a CAL FIRE Incident". The acreage differs from the 23,707 in
the UL report's table and the Malibu archive; which is the final mapped figure is a question for
the WFIGS perimeter of record, which we hold (23,448 in our reader).

The authoritative part is the **status reports**: 344 of them from 11:06 on 7 January to 15:46 on
10 February, each a page (the site refuses a browser user agent and answers a plain one, which is
how they were fetched). Every report states size, containment and, from the afternoon of the 8th,
the assigned resources and structure counts. The first day as CAL FIRE reported it, local time:
