# 2026-11-01 — M2 Heat: two fields, two clocks, one energy ledger across two modules

Same session as M1 (Claude, Fable 5.1, canada1). Seam test 1 of the brief.

## What runs

`manifests/m2-heat-boone.toml`: the sun module at one hour writes its incident energy per
cell into the shared world store; a slab surface module at fifteen minutes reads it,
absorbs (1 - albedo), reflects the rest, radiates to a fixed sky temperature, and declares
two stocks, `ground` (sum of C (T - 273.15 K) area) and `surface` (energy seen but not yet
drained). One energy ledger spans both modules and closes daily. Year 2025 over the Boone
River window at 30 arc-seconds: 43,800 events delivered in 5.7 s, 365 closes, worst leak
648 J against a tolerance of 1 MJ on sums near 1e17 J per day. `esim verify` passes
(same digest twice, exact replay from a snapshot) and two integration tests cover M1 and M2
when the DEM tile is in the cache.

## The ledger earned its keep

The first run leaked 2.2e9 J, two parts in a billion. Two causes, both precision: the sun
transferred an f64 sum while publishing f32 cells, so the heat module could never drain
exactly what the sun had credited; and the heat module kept temperature in f32, so its
recomputed stock drifted from the flows. Neither would have been noticed by looking at a
chart. The fix is a rule for every module from here: account the number you publish, in the
precision you publish it, and keep stocks in f64.

no air and a 255 K sky must be. The seam works; the climate waits for weather (M4).

## Kernel additions

- `World`: shared fields on grids, owned by the first writer, written only through the
  context. Each write is logged as a `world.write` event with the field name, version and a
  digest of the data, not the data itself, so the log stays small and still attests to
  state. The world is in snapshots, so replay is exact.
- `schedule_with_priority`: a reader runs after its writer at equal instants. This is the
  single-threaded form of the conservative rule; the parallel form later reads the same
  ordering.

## Viewer

Maps section landed (subagent, checked by eye in this session): field frames drawn on a
canvas at true aspect for the latitude, one sequential ramp in both themes, a time slider
for multi-frame fields, hover readout of lat, lon and value, legend with the field's
year-wide min and max. The elevation map shows the Boone River's valley network in the
south-west of the window. Known limitation: a field with a strong annual cycle (irradiance,
temperature) looks uniform on any one day under a year-wide scale; a per-frame stretch
toggle is the follow-up.
