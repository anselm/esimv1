# 2026-11-01 — M5 Green: a crop as a field, a carbon ledger, scored against MODIS

Overnight, same session (Claude, Fable 5.1, canada1).

## What runs

`manifests/m5-green-boone.toml`: ERA5 weather, the sun, the basin water module and a new
`veg.crop` module over the Boone River basin at 30 arc-seconds, year 2025, 17 s. One plant
functional type, a summer annual crop standing for corn and soybean together, on every
basin cell:

- planting when five consecutive days average 10 C after day 110; emergence after 80
  growing-degree days as a seedling canopy of leaf area 0.3 from imported seed carbon;
  growth by light-use efficiency on the sun's energy (1.6 g C per MJ of PAR, half
  respired), limited by soil water read from the water module's new `water.soil_mm`
  field and by temperature; senescence from 1,300 to 1,650 degree-days; harvest at
  1,650 or first frost, with 45% of biomass exported as grain and the rest to litter;
  litter respiring with temperature.
- leaf area published as `veg.lai` and read by the water module, whose evaporation now
  scales with canopy cover (the two-way feedback of the brief: soil water limits the crop,
  the crop sets the evaporative demand).
- NDVI from leaf area by a saturating relation, set to zero where the water module's new
  `water.snow_mm` field shows snow.
- a carbon ledger in kg C: atmosphere, import (seed), biomass, litter, export (grain).
  Stocks biomass and litter declared daily. Year 2025: 365 closes, worst leak 1.3e-5 kgC.
  The water and energy ledgers close beside it.

## The scoreboard and its hole

MODIS MOD13A1 (16-day, 500 m NDVI) from Microsoft's Planetary Computer: STAC search,
one anonymous container token per run (per-file signing was rate-limited at 429 after a
handful of requests), COGs cached by item id, sampled from the sinusoidal grid to the
basin cells by the forward projection formula. Our GeoTIFF reader took the tiles as they
are (Int16, deflate, 512-pixel blocks).

The catalog has 29 composites for 2025 over tile h11v04, from both Terra and Aqua, but
none between 27 June and 17 November. Checked for 13A1, 13Q1, 13A2 and 13A3: the same gap.
So the scoreboard misses the peak and the senescence of the growing season. That is the
catalog's hole, not ours; the fix is a second source (Sentinel-2 NDVI through Earth Search
is anonymous and was checked to exist for this basin at 10 m) or LP DAAC with a login.

Basin-mean NDVI, 29 composites, simulated against MODIS:

| NSE | KGE | r | bias | sim peak | MODIS peak (by 27 June) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.456 | 0.498 | 0.835 | -31% | 0.48 on 27 June | 0.78 |

The shape is right and the timing is late. MODIS greens from early April (grass,
roadsides, pasture, winter cover: a 500 m pixel is not all crop) while the model's canopy
starts in mid May and reaches NDVI 0.48 by the end of June, when MODIS is at 0.78. Leaf
area peaks at 3.8 on 31 August, about a month after a real Corn Belt canopy. Biomass
peaks at 322 g C/m2, roughly half of a real corn crop. Three parameters would move all of
this (planting rule, emergence leaf area, light-use efficiency), and moving them by hand
is exactly what the method says not to do; M12's calibration does it against the whole
record.

Two smaller honest findings. First, where the model has snow on 19 February and 7 March
it reports NDVI 0, while MODIS reports 0.19 and 0.22: 16-day maximum-value compositing
picks the snow-free day, so a snow-aware NDVI should compare against the composite's best
day, not its mean. Second, with a real canopy the water module's evaporation changed and
the discharge score moved (NSE 0.11, bias +51% with the coupling, against 0.25 without):
the bare-soil factor of 0.5 is too low for a spring Iowa field. The coupling works; the
constant is a guess and is marked as one.

## Why this rung matters

Populations are the second representation in the store (fields were the first). The
crop is a population with fractional cover and phenology, it reads two fields written by
other modules and writes one that a third module reads, and a third ledger closes across
the seam. The MODIS reader is the registry's first satellite product, and it exposed that
the reference catalogs themselves have gaps a product must plan around.
