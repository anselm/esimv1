# 2026-11-01 — M7 Tearing: the hazard table runs, fire as the first hazard

Overnight, same session (Claude, Fable 5.1, canada1).

## What was built

- `data/events/hazards.toml` is now read by code. `EventTable` loads a table, finds a
  row by id, and can filter rows by `known_since` for honest hindcasts. A new row,
  `cropland-fire-corn-belt`, is marked speculative with a guessed rate, as it should be.
- `hazard.fire`, the first hazard module. It takes its row from the table and logs it
  (`hazard.loaded`, with title, setting, confidence and source), so the run's log says
  where the hazard came from. The brief's three settings work: `off`; `on`, where each
  dry, fuelled, warm cell ignites with a small probability drawn from the module's own
  seeded stream, so ensemble branches sample the hazard; and `forced`, at a date and a
  point, for what-ifs.
- Ignition is the kernel's first sparse cell-addressed event. The herbivore module
  subscribed at every basin cell with `subscribe_at`; when the ignition event arrives at
  one cell, most of that cell's animals move to its neighbours, exactly (counts
  conserved). Spread is a daily cellular automaton: a burning cell lights each neighbour
  with a probability rising with fuel, dryness, temperature and wind. `fire.burn`
  publishes a burned-fraction field; the crop sends 90% of standing biomass and 70% of
  litter in burned cells to the atmosphere on the carbon ledger, and the cohorts lose
  30% of their animals there (to `deaths_fire` on the population ledger).
- A first version burned one cell and never spread: the spread step ran after the
  one-day burn had already moved to its refractory state. The order is now burn today,
  spread to tomorrow, then advance. The integration test asserts that a forced fire
  spreads beyond its cell, emits carbon, reaches the cohort through the spatial index,
  and leaves both the hazard row and a cell-addressed ignition in the log.

## The what-if

`manifests/m7-fire-boone.toml`: the M6 stack plus the fire, forced on 5 September 2025 at
42.75 N 93.85 W, near peak crop biomass. With the default spread parameter (0.35) the
fire dies in its cell: the per-neighbour probability on that day is about 0.09, which is
subcritical, and that is plausibly Iowa. The manifest therefore states an extreme-day
assumption in one line (spread 0.9) and gets:

| | |
|---|---|
| burning days | 10 (5 to 14 September) |
| burned | 14.5 km2, 0.7% of the basin |
| carbon to the atmosphere | 5,819 t C |
| deer fled the ignition cell | 3 |
| deer killed by fire | 19 |
| basin-mean biomass | 321 to 319 g C/m2 |
| ledgers | animals, carbon, energy, water all closed, 365 times |

Nothing here is calibrated and the spread rates are not Rothermel's; the point is that a
hazard from the table changed three other modules' ledgers through two events and one
field, and the run still closes and replays.

## The stochastic setting

`manifests/m7-fire-on-boone.toml` as an 8-branch ensemble, 21 s: one ignition across
eight years of branches (seed 7, 0.6 km2). The seven branches without a fire share one
log digest, so the ensemble reports 2 distinct digests among 8: the only randomness in
the whole stack is the hazard's, and the machinery shows it. At the guessed rate the
hazard is rare, which matches the row's own confidence.

## What is still missing from "tearing"

The brief's thresholds are state variables that cross a line and fire a discrete event
(a forest becomes savanna, a town stops being a town). Fire ignition is a probabilistic
threshold; the deterministic kind, a zero-crossing detector in the kernel, is not built.
The cheapest honest version is a flood threshold on the water module's channel storage
that publishes a cell-addressed `flood` event; it is listed, not done.
